# Writing questions to the agency

The question window is the only time the terms can change without a fight. Use it well.

## Rules
1. **One issue per question.** Number them; the agency answers by number.
2. **Cite the place**: section, page, and up to ten words in quotes. Precision gets answers.
3. **State the conflict or gap neutrally**, then ask. "Section A says one year plus two
   options; Section B says five years. Which applies?" — not "your RFP is wrong".
4. **Give the assumption you will price to if unanswered.** It shows you read closely, it is
   easy for the officer to confirm, and it lets you price defensibly.
5. **Do not reveal strategy, weakness or price.** No "we are a new company"; no internal
   exposure (tax, ownership, insurance history).
6. **Do not assert law you have not read.** Ask: "Does the agency consider X applicable?"
7. **Prefer questions whose answers are public.** Answers usually go to all bidders as an
   addendum; a question that only helps you may also help competitors.
8. **Keep them few.** Twenty-five good questions get answered; sixty get skimmed.

## Priority order (what changes price or eligibility)
Labor standards and wage rates · how price is scored and on which quantities · who furnishes
which materials · term and price adjustment · volume and not-to-exceed · bonds · partial or
regional awards · environmental hazards (lead, asbestos) by property · service-level
definitions · insurance items that may be unobtainable · who may sign and with what
authority · current form editions · reporting formats · submission mechanics.

## Template
> **Q7.** *Reference:* Sample Contract §2, PDF p. 66, "not to exceed … per year". *Question:*
> The amount is blank. What is the not-to-exceed amount and estimated annual volume?
> *Assumption if not answered:* no minimum volume is guaranteed and prices are firm for the
> initial term.

## Also consider
A public-information request for the prior solicitation's bid tabulation and award records
(a separate communication the owner decides to send) — it gives pricing context the RFP
does not. Name the statute only with a verify flag until read.

## After the answers
Read the addendum line by line; update the register; re-check every assumption you priced to;
acknowledge the addendum exactly as the solicitation requires (late or missing acknowledgements
have disqualified bidders).
